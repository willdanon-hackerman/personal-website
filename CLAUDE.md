# William Danon — Personal Website

## Purpose

This is William Danon's personal website.

It should function as a personal home on the internet rather than a conventional professional portfolio or resume site.

The website is an ongoing collection of projects, films, writing, notes, interests, research, and experiments. It should be able to evolve as William's interests and work change.

Do not try to summarize William with a professional label such as "entrepreneur," "filmmaker," "creative," or "multidisciplinary storyteller." Let the work and content explain who he is.

## Design Philosophy

The website should combine two sensibilities:

1. The simplicity, directness, and personality of the older internet.
2. Strong contemporary photography and visual storytelling.

The website itself should feel relatively simple and handmade. The content—particularly photography and video—can be visually rich.

The goal is NOT to make a polished agency portfolio, startup landing page, or fashionable Webflow template.

It should feel like a website that an interesting person has maintained and added to over time.

## Visual Language

Prefer:

- generous whitespace
- strong typography
- simple navigation
- visible text links
- underlined links where appropriate
- horizontal rules
- dates and captions
- straightforward lists
- editorial layouts
- large photography
- documentary imagery
- occasional dense information
- restrained use of color
- layouts that feel slightly archival or publication-like

Potential color influences include:

- cream
- black
- navy
- oxblood
- forest green
- weathered red

Color should be restrained rather than decorative.

Possible visual references include:

- old personal websites
- local newspapers
- college newspapers
- field guides
- old sporting magazines
- community bulletin boards
- film contact sheets
- 1980s–1990s outdoor catalogs
- northeastern coastal towns
- English country clothing catalogs

## Avoid

Do NOT default to common contemporary portfolio or AI-generated website aesthetics.

Avoid:

- SaaS-style design
- gradients
- excessive rounded corners
- floating pills
- card grids
- glassmorphism
- unnecessary icons
- giant generic hero statements
- excessive animation
- scroll effects for their own sake
- generic startup typography
- marketing copy
- buzzwords
- overly polished agency aesthetics
- labeling William as a "storyteller," "creative," "entrepreneur," etc.

Do not add functionality or sections merely to make the website appear more sophisticated.

## Writing Style

Copy should sound like a person rather than a brand.

Prefer:

"I'm William Danon. I live in New York."

over:

"William Danon is a multidisciplinary storyteller exploring the intersection of culture, entrepreneurship, and human connection."

Writing should be:

- direct
- specific
- curious
- understated
- occasionally informal

Avoid marketing language and unnecessary self-description.

## Development Principles

Keep the codebase simple.

Use the existing Next.js, TypeScript, and Tailwind setup.

Do not introduce new dependencies unless they provide a clear benefit.

Prefer straightforward implementations over clever abstractions.

Keep components readable.

Maintain responsive behavior for desktop and mobile.

When making substantial design changes, explain the proposed approach before implementing it.

Do not expand the scope of a task without being asked.
